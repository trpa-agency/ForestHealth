# References for the Caldor reforestation need analysis

Gathered Oct. 11, 2026. The 150 m seed source cutoff and the patch size framing trace to
the sources below. Quotes are short; read the papers before citing them in a memo.

## The 150 m figure

**Haffey, C., T.D. Sisk, C.D. Allen, A.E. Thode, and E.Q. Margolis. 2018.** Limits to ponderosa
pine regeneration following large high-severity forest fires in the United States Southwest.
Fire Ecology 14(1):143-163. https://doi.org/10.4996/fireecology.140114316

This is the paper that states the 150 m threshold. The abstract says areas more than
150 m from a seed source "are much less likely to have ponderosa pine regeneration."
Distance to seed source was the strongest predictor in the model. The median distance to a
seed source where natural regeneration occurred was 67 m, against 124 m for all plots.
Southwest ponderosa pine, not Sierra mixed conifer, so the number transfers as a planning
rule rather than a measured Tahoe response.

## Sierra Nevada mixed conifer regeneration and seed distance

**Welch, K.R., H.D. Safford, and T.P. Young. 2016.** Predicting conifer establishment post
wildfire in mixed conifer forests of the North American Mediterranean-climate zone.
Ecosphere 7(12):e01609. https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.1609

1,490 plots on 14 fires across 10 California national forests. Distance to the nearest
potential seed tree was recorded in five classes: 0-30, 30-60, 60-120, 120-200, and more
than 200 m (the laser range finder limit, so high severity plots with no visible seed tree
were entered as 200 m). Conifer seedling density fell consistently with each distance
class. No single cutoff is given.

**Shive, K.L., H.K. Preisler, K.R. Welch, H.D. Safford, J.J. Bellows, K.L. O'Hara, and S.L.
Stephens. 2018.** From the stand scale to the landscape scale: predicting the spatial
patterns of forest regeneration after disturbance. Ecological Applications 28(6):1626-1639.
https://doi.org/10.1002/eap.1756

The POSCRPT model. Euclidean distance to seed source is an input, evaluated from 50 to
500 m.

**Stewart, J.A.E., P.J. van Mantgem, D.J.N. Young, K.L. Shive, H.K. Preisler, A.J. Das, N.L.
Stephenson, J.E. Keeley, H.D. Safford, M.C. Wright, K.R. Welch, and J.H. Thorne. 2021.**
Effects of postfire climate and seed availability on postfire conifer regeneration.
Ecological Applications 31(3):e02280. https://doi.org/10.1002/eap.2280

Fits seed dispersal kernels by species group. Best fits had mean dispersal distances of
28, 36, and 116 m, with 95 percent of seed falling within about 68, 88, and 284 m of the
source. Regeneration can still occur at low levels far from seed sources through rare
long-distance dispersal.

**Stevens-Rumann, C.S., and P. Morgan. 2019.** Tree regeneration following wildfires in the
western US: a review. Fire Ecology 15:15. https://doi.org/10.1186/s42408-019-0032-1

Review with a table of seed source distances by study; lists Welch et al. 2016 (Pacific
Southwest) at about 40 m and Haffey et al. 2018 (Southwest) at 150 m. Not fetched in
full (publisher login wall); confirm the table before quoting it.

**Chambers, M.E., P.J. Fornwalt, S.L. Malone, and M.A. Battaglia. 2016.** Patterns of conifer
regeneration following high severity wildfire in ponderosa pine-dominated forests of the
Colorado Front Range. Forest Ecology and Management 378:57-67.

Front Range: about 170 regenerating conifer stems per hectare within 50 m of the forest
edge, fewer than 50 per hectare at 150 m.

## TRPA's large patch standard (why 200 acres is a break)

**Tahoe Regional Planning Agency. 2026.** Forest Health Threshold Standards Update, Landscape
Fire Dynamics standard. Threshold indicator 192
(https://thresholds.laketahoeinfo.org/threshold/indicators/192), StoryMap
(https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/24a244191f6842c5ab44bc09e228cf6b), full report on Box
(https://trpa.app.box.com/s/9wksrvupifkzhrvtc6tlbltonnjijyfc).

The standard: limit modeled high severity fire patches of 200 acres or greater to less
than five percent of the landscape outside urban areas and designated wilderness, under
90th percentile fire weather. Current condition 8.9 percent (14,176 of 157,843 acres),
Considerably Worse than Target. The report's reasoning, from the repo's `index.md`:
high severity patches "are of particular concern when they become large, because they
eliminate seed sources and can permanently convert forest stands to shrub fields. Natural
regeneration typically fails when the distance to a live seed tree exceeds approximately
120 meters. By allowing patches up to 200 acres, the standard acknowledges that some
high-severity fire supports habitat diversity while setting a ceiling to prevent
landscape-scale ecological degradation." It also notes the Caldor Fire burned "roughly
5,000 acres in large, high-severity patches that closely aligned with predicted hazard
outputs."

Why it is in this analysis: 200 acres is the Region's own definition of a large high
severity patch, so the patch size classes break there (with 10 and 100 acres from
GTR-270), and the Basin acres in patches of 200 acres or more are the Caldor acres that
count against the standard. The report's 120 m seed distance is also carried as a band in
the app next to Haffey's 150 m.

## Reforestation guidance (the GTRs)

**Long, J.W., D. Walsh, M. Coppoletta, R.E. Tompkins, M.D. Meyer, C. Isbell, G.N. Bohlman,
and M.P. North. 2023.** Interventions to restore wildfire-altered forests in California.
Gen. Tech. Rep. PSW-GTR-278. Albany, CA: USDA Forest Service, Pacific Southwest Research
Station. 105 p. https://doi.org/10.2737/PSW-GTR-278

The replanting GTR. Companion to GTR-270; covers specific treatments. Suggested
strategies include "targeting interiors of very large patches of high severity for
harvest and replanting," fuel reduction in moderate and low severity patches, treating
ridgelines, and encouraging beneficial fire. Contents include Planting Conifers (p. 25)
and Zone 1: Uncharacteristically Large, High-Severity Burn Patches (p. 40). PDF:
https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/66673.pdf (34 MB).

On seed distance (Zone 1 discussion): "To inform decisions about replanting, several
threshold values for distance to seed trees have been suggested for mixed-conifer forests
in recent publications, including 60, 120, and 200 m. The 120-m range seems to have become
a rule of thumb for managers, being cited in an example from the King Fire (North et al.
2019) and used in an analysis of the Rough Fire (Meyer et al. 2021)." It adds that North
et al. 2019 used 200 m as a high-end rule to capture any natural regeneration, Clark et al.
1999 suggested 100 m for wind dispersal in mixed conifer, and McDonald 1980 found more than
90 percent of conifer seed within one and a half dominant tree heights (about 60 m). The
150 m figure is not in this GTR; it sits between the 120 m rule of thumb and the 200 m
high-end rule.

## Caldor Fire restoration decision

**USDA Forest Service, Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit. 2026.** Caldor Fire Restoration
Project, Environmental Assessment and Decision Notice. Decision signed Mar. 27, 2026,
under an Emergency Action Determination; about 11,700 acres of national forest land.
Scoping Apr. 4, 2025; draft EA comment period opened Dec. 5, 2025.
- Project page: https://www.fs.usda.gov/r05/laketahoebasin/projects/65228
- Project documents (Pinyon Public): https://usfs-public.app.box.com/v/PinyonPublic/folder/235777475555
- Reading room: https://cara.fs2c.usda.gov/Public/ReadingRoom?Project=65228
- LT Info EIP project record: https://www.laketahoeinfo.org/Project/Detail/4392
Proposed work per the news coverage: thinning, dead tree removal, site preparation for
native seedlings, targeted herbicide for reforestation, prescribed fire, and stream,
meadow, and aspen restoration. The project area polygon would be a useful overlay for
this app; it was not fetched.

**Meyer, M.D., J.W. Long, and H.D. Safford, eds. 2021.** Postfire restoration framework for
national forests in California. Gen. Tech. Rep. PSW-GTR-270. Albany, CA: USDA Forest
Service, Pacific Southwest Research Station. 204 p.

Chapter 4 (Estes, Meyer, Gross, Walsh, and Isbell), mixed conifer case study: "In larger
high-severity burned patches, much of the burned area can be far from available seed
sources," and "uncharacteristically large high-severity patches (especially those
exceeding about 100 ac [40 ha]) could result in tree regeneration failure." Patch size
classes used: under 10 ac, 10 to 100 ac, 100 to 250 ac, over 250 ac. Natural range of
variation: mean high severity patch under 10 ac, large patches rarely over 247 ac.

**North, M.P., J.T. Stevens, D.F. Greene, M. Coppoletta, E.E. Knapp, A.M. Latimer, C.M.
Restaino, R.E. Tompkins, K.R. Welch, R.A. York, D.J.N. Young, J.N. Axelson, T.N. Buckley,
B.L. Estes, R.N. Hager, J.W. Long, M.D. Meyer, S.M. Ostoja, H.D. Safford, K.L. Shive, C.L.
Tubbesing, H. Vice, D. Walsh, C.M. Werner, and P. Wyrsch. 2019.** Tamm Review:
Reforestation for resilience in dry western U.S. forests. Forest Ecology and Management
432:209-224. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foreco.2018.09.007

Not a GTR. The design paper for clustered, variable density planting after fire.
