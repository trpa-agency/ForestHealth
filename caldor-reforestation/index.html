Frame

The 2021 Caldor Fire incident perimeter clipped to the TRPA boundary, on a 30 m grid in NAD83 UTM Zone 10N. Every acre figure on this page is inside the Basin unless it says otherwise. Replanting need is only asked of pixels that were forest before the fire: a conifer vegetation type, or at least 25 percent canopy cover in summer 2020. Chaparral and barren ground that burned at high severity appear in the severity chart but not in the need figures.

Severity

Vegetation burn severity is the four class product published on the TRPA Fire map service (unburned or very low, low, moderate, high). Soil burn severity is the Burned Area Emergency Response team's final map of Sept. 14, 2021. "High" is class 4 of each. The overlap is the pixelwise intersection.

Patches

Patches are eight connected groups of high vegetation severity pixels, delineated across the whole fire so a patch that crosses the Basin boundary keeps its full size. Size classes break at 10, 100, and 200 acres. The 200 acre break is TRPA's adopted Landscape Fire Dynamics standard, which limits modeled high severity patches of 200 acres or more to less than five percent of the landscape outside urban areas and wilderness; the threshold report explains that large patches "eliminate seed sources and can permanently convert forest stands to shrub fields" while patches under 200 acres still support habitat diversity. The 10 and 100 acre breaks follow PSW-GTR-270, which flags patches over about 100 acres as at risk of regeneration failure. Patch depth is the distance from the nearest pixel that is not high severity, which says how far the interior sits from any edge.

Seed sources and distance

A seed source pixel burned at unburned or very low or low severity (or lies outside the perimeter), carried at least 25 percent canopy cover in summer 2020, and is a conifer CWHR type. Moderate severity does not count. Distance is Euclidean distance to the nearest seed source pixel, computed over the perimeter plus 600 m so the fire edge and the Basin boundary do not truncate it.

The 150 m cutoff comes from Haffey and others (2018, Fire Ecology), who found that areas more than 150 m from a seed source were much less likely to regenerate after large high severity fires in Southwest ponderosa pine. TRPA's Forest Health Threshold Standards report uses 120 m, the figure PSW-GTR-278 calls the managers' rule of thumb for mixed conifer, and both are offered in the selector. Sierra Nevada studies (Welch, Safford, and Young 2016; Stewart and others 2021) show the same steep decline with distance, with most seed falling within about 70 to 280 m of the source depending on species.

Limits

  • Severity is as mapped in 2021. Salvage, planting, and natural regeneration since are not reflected.
  • The published vegetation severity layer does not say which RAVG metric it is (composite burn index, basal area loss, or canopy cover loss); "high" differs slightly among them.
  • Ownership comes from parcels; roads and unparceled land show as Unknown.

Planting guidance: USDA Forest Service PSW-GTR-278, Interventions to Restore Wildfire-Altered Forests in California (Long and others 2023), and PSW-GTR-270, Postfire Restoration Framework for National Forests in California (Meyer, Long, and Safford 2021), which flags high severity patches over about 100 acres as at risk of regeneration failure.

Seed source distance

  • Haffey, C., T.D. Sisk, C.D. Allen, A.E. Thode, and E.Q. Margolis. 2018. Limits to ponderosa pine regeneration following large high-severity forest fires in the United States Southwest. Fire Ecology 14(1):143-163. doi:10.4996/fireecology.140114316. Source of the 150 m cutoff: areas more than 150 m from a seed source "are much less likely" to regenerate.
  • Welch, K.R., H.D. Safford, and T.P. Young. 2016. Predicting conifer establishment post wildfire in mixed conifer forests of the North American Mediterranean-climate zone. Ecosphere 7(12):e01609. doi:10.1002/ecs2.1609. 1,490 plots on 14 California fires; seedling density fell with every distance class from 0 to 30 m out past 200 m.
  • Stewart, J.A.E., and others. 2021. Effects of postfire climate and seed availability on postfire conifer regeneration. Ecological Applications 31(3):e02280. doi:10.1002/eap.2280. Dispersal kernels: 95 percent of seed within about 68 to 284 m of the source, by species group.
  • Shive, K.L., and others. 2018. From the stand scale to the landscape scale: predicting the spatial patterns of forest regeneration after disturbance. Ecological Applications 28(6):1626-1639. doi:10.1002/eap.1756. The POSCRPT model.
  • Stevens-Rumann, C.S., and P. Morgan. 2019. Tree regeneration following wildfires in the western US: a review. Fire Ecology 15:15. doi:10.1186/s42408-019-0032-1.

Reforestation guidance

  • Long, J.W., D. Walsh, M. Coppoletta, R.E. Tompkins, M.D. Meyer, C. Isbell, G.N. Bohlman, and M.P. North. 2023. Interventions to restore wildfire-altered forests in California. Gen. Tech. Rep. PSW-GTR-278. USDA Forest Service, Pacific Southwest Research Station. doi:10.2737/PSW-GTR-278. Reviews seed distance thresholds of 60, 120, and 200 m and notes that 120 m "seems to have become a rule of thumb for managers."
  • Meyer, M.D., J.W. Long, and H.D. Safford, eds. 2021. Postfire restoration framework for national forests in California. Gen. Tech. Rep. PSW-GTR-270. Treesearch 62871. Chapter 4 flags high severity patches over about 100 acres as at risk of regeneration failure; patch classes of under 10, 10 to 100, 100 to 250, and over 250 acres.
  • North, M.P., and others. 2019. Tamm Review: Reforestation for resilience in dry western U.S. forests. Forest Ecology and Management 432:209-224. doi:10.1016/j.foreco.2018.09.007.

TRPA threshold standard

  • Tahoe Regional Planning Agency. 2026. Forest Health Threshold Standards Update, Landscape Fire Dynamics standard: high severity patches of 200 acres or more on less than five percent of the assessed landscape. Threshold indicator 192, StoryMap, and the full report. The report's rationale: large patches eliminate seed sources and can convert forest to shrub fields, natural regeneration typically fails beyond about 120 m from a live seed tree, and the Caldor Fire burned roughly 5,000 acres in large high severity patches that matched the modeled hazard.
