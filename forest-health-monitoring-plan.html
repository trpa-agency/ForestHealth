The design

Half the sample answers a question the other half cannot

1
Half 1

Spatially balanced, not stratified

Sites spread evenly across the whole forested frame with no pre-stratification. This keeps the population definition intact as forest gains and loses ground between measurement cycles, which is what lets us report trend honestly in ten years. It is what the national inventory does, and it answers the objection our partners have raised twice.

2
Half 2

Weighted toward where we are blind

Sites selected with unequal probability, weighted toward the structural classes carrying the most reporting uncertainty and toward aspen, riparian, and LiDAR reference types. Weighted rather than hand-picked, so every site keeps an inclusion probability and can still enter an estimate.

One ranked site list, produced with the same method our partners already use, serves both this network and the biodiversity threshold now in development.

Recommended by Pat Manley, September 19, 2026. Resolves the proportional versus tail-weighted question open since August 31.

The protocol

What gets measured, and what it costs

1

Threshold metrics

Every tree: species, diameter, height, crown base, crown ratio, crown width, damage, decay. Plus a hundred-point canopy cover reading and site data.

Every plot, base rate
2

Surface and ground fuels

Four transects from plot center. Coarse and fine woody debris, duff and litter depth, and percent of each piece charred by fire.

Every plot, base rate
3

Understory composition

Cover by growth habit and height layer, and species at three percent cover or more. Remote sensing cannot deliver this at all.

Subset, priced separately

Pricing the understory separately lets us set the trade between how many plots we get and how much we learn at each one, at the moment we sign the contract rather than now.

Why the plot got bigger

Small plots cannot validate remote sensing

Two things break a small plot

Position error. Shift a small plot four metres and it captures an almost entirely different set of LiDAR returns. Our plot keeps 86 percent of its footprint under the same shift.

Crown overhang. The field rule counts a tree by where its trunk is. The LiDAR sees where its crown is. That mismatch halves every time the radius doubles.

Yang et al. 2023, Remote Sensing 15(14):3509. The 0.10 ha value is interpolated. Chirici et al. 2024 recommend against plots under 300 square metres for inventory of any kind.

The problem nobody had specified

The canopy cover number is two different numbers

What the standard means

Draw the outline around a crown and everything inside it is cover, gaps included. This is how the threshold and the national inventory both define it.

What the LiDAR measures

A pulse that falls through a gap between branches is not a hit. The model sees through the crown that the standard treats as solid.

Attainment turns on this. VP9 splits open from closed at 50 percent cover, and 40 percent in Jeffrey pine. Field cover is systematically higher than modeled cover, and the gap is widest in the open-crowned types, which are exactly the types furthest from target. So our crews record both readings at all one hundred points on every plot. The difference stops being an unknown and becomes a number we publish.

Ma et al. 2019 identify definitional differences as the dominant cause of disagreement between LiDAR cover products in the Sierra Nevada.

The money

What the funding actually buys

Where the money is not going

The sampling design, the field protocol, and the data collection app are all built in house. None of them are purchased.

That is the whole reason the funding reaches this many plots. The earlier version of this contract would have spent a large share of it buying a design.

Assumes fixed-price mobilization and handoff tasks come off the top. Real per-plot costs are being pulled from comparable Forest Service contracts.

Roles

Who does what

TRPA

Sampling design, field protocol, Survey123 app, plot navigation packets, monumentation materials, land access coordination, and the survey-grade repositioning of the existing sites.

Contractor

Installs, monuments, positions, and measures the new plots at firm unit rates, performs blind remeasurement on a subset, and delivers relocation documentation any agency can work from.

Partners

Forest Service provides the existing site network, the ranked site list, and protocol review. Nobody advising on the design is bidding on the work.

Doing the design in house is also what makes the network transferable. TRPA owns the protocol and the app from day one, so there is no handoff to negotiate at the end of a contract.
