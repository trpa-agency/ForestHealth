At a glance

How fire is likely to behave in the Basin's forests

Fire is part of how Tahoe forests work. The program tracks whether the fire the Basin would experience today still performs that role: the severity at which the landscape is likely to burn under severe fire weather, conditions in the wildland urban interface (WUI) where firefighters must be able to work, the fuel reduction that changes those conditions, and the fires that actually occur.

Monitoring approach

How fire dynamics are measured

Modeled fire behavior for the whole landscape, observed fire outcomes where fires occur, and treatment accomplishments reported by the agencies that do the work.

Fire behavior modeling

Fire hazard and severity are modeled across the Basin under 90th percentile fire weather using the Pyrologix Wildfire Exposure Simulation Tool, delivered through the Sierra Nevada Regional Resource Kit (2023 vintage). Each 30-meter cell gets a probability of burning at low, moderate, or high severity; the class with the highest probability is mapped.

Landscape fire

Across all vegetation types outside urban areas, designated wilderness, and water (157,843 acres), the program tracks the share of land likely to burn at high severity in contiguous patches of 200 acres or more. Large high-severity patches remove seed sources and expose soil in ways small patches do not.

WUI Defense Zone

The Defense Zone extends roughly a quarter mile from community edges and is where firefighters must be able to engage a fire directly. Its wildland portion outside urban land use is 44,920 acres. The program tracks the share predicted to burn as surface fire, with flame lengths short enough for direct attack, under 90th percentile weather.

Management zones

Modeled severity is summarized by the forest management zones of the Multi-Jurisdictional Fuel Reduction and Wildfire Prevention Strategy: WUI Defense Zone, WUI Threat Zone, General Forest, and the Desolation, Granite Chief, and Mt. Rose wilderness areas.

Fire history and burn severity

Wildfire perimeters from the CAL FIRE statewide fire history dataset, updated yearly. Post-fire vegetation burn severity for large fires, such as the 2021 Caldor Fire, from USFS mapping. Observed severity is how the modeled products will be checked over time.

Treatments

Acres of forest fuels treated, as reported by Tahoe Fire and Fuels Team agencies to the EIP Project Tracker, by treatment zone. Treatment is the main lever that moves modeled severity, so the two are read together.

About the program. The Fire Dynamics monitoring program tracks modeled and observed fire behavior under the Vegetation Preservation threshold category. It supports two threshold standards: Landscape Fire Dynamics and Wildland Urban Interface Defense Zone Fire Dynamics.
