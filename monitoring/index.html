Forest Health Monitoring

Three monitoring programs track the forest health standards of the Vegetation Preservation threshold category for the Lake Tahoe Basin. Each program has a dashboard that answers the monitoring questions: what is measured, how, where, and what the record shows. The dashboards embed in the Dashboard card of their program page on monitoring.laketahoeinfo.org and can also be opened directly from here.

Program

Forest Structure

Seral stage, canopy cover, and stand density of the Basin's non-urban conifer forest, summarized by forest type from 30-meter remote sensing products.

  • Five seral stage and canopy cover classes by forest type
  • Acres within the desired trees per acre and basal area
  • Map of forest types and structure classes
  • Ground calibration plots, as they are established
Supports the Seral Stage and Canopy Cover and Stand Density standards. Data of record: the Forest Health analysis outputs in this repository.

Fire Dynamics

Modeled fire severity under severe fire weather by management zone, conditions in the wildland urban interface, fuel reduction accomplished, and the fires that reach the Basin.

  • Most likely fire severity by management zone
  • Fuel treatments by year and treatment zone
  • Wildfire perimeters and Caldor Fire burn severity
  • Named wildfires since 1980
Supports the Landscape Fire Dynamics and WUI Defense Zone Fire Dynamics standards. Live from the TRPA Fire map service on maps.trpa.org.

Program

Subalpine Conifer

Extent, species composition, and structure of the subalpine forest above about 8,500 feet, and the health of whitebark pine, tracked by remote sensing informed by ground plots.

  • Mapped extent and dominant species
  • Tree size and canopy cover classes
  • Whitebark pine health, as surveys begin
  • Plot network, as it is established
Supports the Subalpine Conifer non-degradation standard. Live from the TRPA Vegetation Type map service on maps.trpa.org.

About these programs

The Forest Health Threshold Standards Update replaced TRPA's legacy vegetation standards with five standards for forest structure, fire dynamics, and subalpine conifer. Four of the five were assessed in 2023 from the Sierra Nevada Regional Resource Kit, a set of remote sensing products covering disturbance through 2022. The standards were developed with the Tahoe Fire and Fuels Team and peer reviewed by the Tahoe Science Advisory Council, which recommended pairing the modeling with on-the-ground monitoring. These dashboards are where that monitoring record accumulates.

The dashboards are descriptive. Attainment status for each standard is evaluated on the threshold indicator pages linked from each dashboard's footer.
