At a glance

Subalpine forest in the Lake Tahoe Basin

Subalpine conifer forest occupies the highest forested ground in the Basin, generally above 8,500 feet, where short growing seasons, thin soils, heavy snowpack, and wind produce open, slow-growing stands of whitebark pine, western white pine, mountain hemlock, and lodgepole pine. These stands were rarely logged and remain largely within their natural range of variation, so the standard for them is non-degradation rather than restoration.

Whitebark pine health

Condition of whitebark pine

Whitebark pine is federally listed as threatened. Across its range it is losing ground to white pine blister rust, mountain pine beetle, and a warming climate that pushes suitable habitat upslope. Plot surveys will track infection, mortality, and regeneration so that change is detected early.

Plot network

Ground-based monitoring plots

Permanent plots measure species composition, tree health, and regeneration on the ground. Those measurements calibrate the remote sensing that tracks the whole subalpine zone between plot visits.

About the program. The Subalpine Conifer monitoring program tracks non-degradation of subalpine conifer communities under the Vegetation Preservation threshold category. The applicable standard protects these communities from degradation while allowing restoration such as seed collection and planting of rust-resistant whitebark pine seedlings.
